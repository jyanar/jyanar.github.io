jorge yanar

Hello! I'm a PhD student and NSF GRFP Fellow at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, working in the lab of Carlos Brody. I study perceptual decision making and working memory in mice and rats. Their genetic accessibility and amenability to high throughput training methods makes them wonderful models to work with.

Before coming to Princeton, I was a post-baccalaureate student in the Brain & Cognitive Sciences department at MIT. There I worked in the labs of Earl Miller and Michael Halassa. I studied the role of the thalamocortical system in maintaining anesthetic and conscious states in non-human primates. Before that, I was at Loyola University Chicago, where I studied markers of human brain aging with Robert G. Morrison and did some physics.
